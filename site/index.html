A calm reader for the Feeds you follow.

Feedrico is a desktop RSS reader for Linux, Windows and macOS. It shows the new Articles from the sites you choose in one chronological list — no algorithm in the way, no account, nothing phoning home. Everything stays in one Library folder on your machine.

What Feedrico promises.

Four things, said plainly.

  • Your order, not an algorithm’s

    Articles arrive in one chronological list, in the order the Feeds publish them. Nothing is ranked, recommended or hidden, and there are no streaks or badges pulling at your attention.

  • Reads offline

    Images are saved when Feeds refresh, so on a train or a plane everything is already there. Opening an Article never makes a network request.

  • Fast at any size

    Twenty Feeds or two thousand: the list scrolls at full speed, and search covers every Article in your Library.

  • No account, because there’s no server

    There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to log into. Your reading is not a profile on somebody else’s machine.

  • Private by design

    Reading makes no network request, so no one learns what you read. Here is everyone Feedrico talks to.

  • Free, no subscription

    When version 1 ships it will be a free download — no paid tier, no ads. The source is public under the GPL-3.0, so the promise is checkable.

One window, four Layouts.

Feeds and Folders on the left, Articles in the middle, the reader on the right. Each Layout suits a different kind of reading: a quick List for news, Cards or Magazine for a slow morning, Gallery for photo Feeds. Every View remembers the Layout you chose for it; Feedrico never guesses for you.

The reader sets the Feed’s own text at a comfortable measure, with its images already on your disk — so reading works with the Wi-Fi off.

Your reading goes where you go.

Everything you read lives in one folder, the Library: your Feeds and Folders, every Article, what you’ve Read and Starred, the images, your settings. Copy the folder to a USB stick and open it on another computer — your reading travels with you.

Portable Mode runs Feedrico from the stick itself, so nothing is installed on the other machine. It works with the Linux AppImage and the portable Windows app; macOS doesn’t allow apps to run from removable drives.

  1. At home

    150 Feeds in 8 Folders, a few thousand Articles and one Starred essay, all inside a folder called Feedrico Library.

  2. In a pocket

    The Library is ordinary files. Copy the folder to a USB stick with any file manager; there is no export step and no account to sign out of.

  3. On the train

    No signal, and nothing missing. Images were saved when the Feeds last refreshed, and opening an Article makes no network request at all.

  4. At a friend’s

    Plug the stick in and run Feedrico from it. Nothing gets installed, and your friend’s own Library stays exactly as it was.

  5. As you left it

    The same Article, still Starred, still Read, in the Layout you chose for it. Your settings travel too. Back home, the folder goes back where it came from.

Your data stays on your machine.

Everything Feedrico saves — your Feeds, Articles, what you’ve Read and Starred, the images, your settings — lives in one folder on your computer. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, and there is no account to sign into. It’s your reading, on your disk: copy it, back it up, take it with you.

Any backup tool or USB stick works — there is no export step and nothing hidden in a system directory.

That’s everyone.

Reading makes no requests. When you open an Article, its images come from your Library, so a tracking pixel never learns that you read it. Scripts, forms and embedded frames are stripped from Feed content before it reaches the reader; a video becomes a plain link you can open in your browser. Links open only when you click them.

There is no account, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no font or script fetched from anywhere else. This page plays by the same rule, and counts its own requests at the bottom.

If you watch your network traffic while you read, you’ll see nothing. We’d like you to check.

Where to start, depending on who you are.

“I’ve never used a feed reader.”
Import the export file from your old reader, or paste the address of a website you like — Feedrico finds its Feed. You can be reading in two minutes, and every empty screen says what to do next.
“I follow over a thousand Feeds, and half of them are dead.”
The list stays smooth, search covers every Article, and Starred Articles are kept forever. Broken Feeds get a quiet badge and are retried later, at longer and longer intervals.
“I have fifteen minutes and a coffee.”
Open Today in the Magazine Layout. There are no streaks and no badges asking for attention. When you’re through, it says so and lets you go.

“A friend wants to plug in their stick.”

Questions people ask.

Short answers, no fine print.

What is an RSS reader?
An RSS reader — also called a feed reader — follows the websites, blogs and newsletters you choose and shows their new articles in one list. You pick the sources; no algorithm decides what you see.
Does Feedrico work offline?
Yes. Images are saved when Feeds refresh, and opening an Article makes no network request, so everything you’ve fetched is readable on a train or a plane.
Does Feedrico have an account or sync?
No. There is no account, no sign-up and no sync service. Everything lives in one Library folder on your machine; copy it to a USB stick to take your reading to another computer.
Can I import my Feeds from another reader?
Yes. Import the OPML file your current reader exports, or paste the address of a website you like — Feedrico finds its Feed.
Is Feedrico free? Is it open source?
Yes and yes. When version 1 is released it will be a free download for Linux, Windows and macOS — no subscription, no paid tier — and the source is public under the GPL-3.0.
Does Feedrico track me?
No telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting. Feedrico talks only to the Feeds you follow when they refresh, and to GitHub if you click Check for updates. This page counts its own requests at the bottom, and you can check.

Get Feedrico.

Feedrico isn’t released yet. Version 1 is still being built, so there is nothing to download today — and no waiting list to join. We’d rather say so plainly.

When version 1 is ready, it will be a free download on GitHub: an AppImage and a .deb for Linux, an installer and a portable app for Windows, a .dmg for macOS. Watch the repository and it will appear here first; there is no newsletter.
